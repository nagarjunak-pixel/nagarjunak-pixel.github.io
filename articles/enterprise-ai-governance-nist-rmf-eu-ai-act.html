Inside an Enterprise AI Governance Council: Practical Scale, Fix, or Retire Decisions Under NIST AI RMF & the EU AI Act

Published November 16, 2026 · Nominated AI Governance Council Member (John Deere)

NIST AI RMF (Govern · Map · Measure · Manage) EU AI Act Risk Tiering Scale / Fix / Retire Portfolio Discipline

1. Governance That Accelerates Production Rather Than Blocking It

Paper-only AI governance boards fail in two directions: either they rubber-stamp unmeasured prototypes that later hallucinate in front of customers, or they paralyze engineering teams with abstract legal checklists disconnected from CI/CD pipelines. Serving as a nominated member on John Deere's AI Governance Council while simultaneously leading a 12+ member delivery team across 23+ AI initiatives taught me that effective governance must be expressed as executable architectural controls.

2. Four Executable Controls Mapped to NIST AI RMF & EU AI Act

  • MAP — Explicit Autonomy & Risk Tiering: Classify every workflow before build: read-only search/summarization, human-reviewed copilot drafting, confidence-gated ticket triage (e.g., automating ~25% of tickets at ~83% accuracy while routing uncertain cases to specialists), or system-of-record mutation.
  • MEASURE — Release-Blocking Quantitative Benchmarks: Require an automated evaluation gate (such as our 255-check AIFlow suite) and live drift/cost/latency telemetry before any pilot graduates to production.
  • MANAGE — Fresh Human Approval & Audit Trails: Enforce in code — never merely in system prompts — that agents cannot alter official enterprise records without a fresh human approval and immutable event logging.
  • GOVERN — Quarterly Scale, Fix, or Retire Decisions: Evaluate every AI initiative against three hard numbers: verified quality score, active user adoption / hours saved, and AWS FinOps unit cost. Initiatives stuck in low accuracy get re-architected (like our ~30% → ~70% agent workflow rescue) or cleanly retired so engineering capacity compounds on winners.
